# Fortress v2026:OCT:03-10:54 — the HL Bank loan

## What the approval closed

The car loan was the largest hole left in Fortress. The invoice called it only
"Bank Financing"; the sales agreement left *Hire Purchase Amount* and *Finance Company*
blank. For three builds the app held the principal and said, in those words, that it knew
nothing else.

| | |
|---|---|
| Lender | **HL Bank** (applied through Vantage Automotive Limited) |
| Principal | S$105,000.00 |
| Rate | **2.28% flat** |
| Tenor | **84 months** (7 years) |
| Instalment | **S$1,450.00** as the bank states it |

The purchase price on the approval, **S$179,388**, matches the invoice to the dollar.
That is what lets these terms be attached to *this car* rather than merely to an
application.

## The finding that matters

**2.28% flat is 4.29% in real money.**

A flat rate charges interest on the original S$105,000 for all seven years, even though
you have repaid most of it by the end. Solving for the reducing-balance rate that produces
the same instalment gives **4.2945%** — 2.01 points above the headline.

| what you owe | rate |
|---|---|
| UOB mortgage | 1.13% |
| DBS margin loan | 2.15% |
| **HL Bank car loan** | **4.29%** |

This is the dearest money you owe apart from the MCST's 12% late-payment charge. The card
says so, and says it as arithmetic on your own figures — bisection on the annuity formula
until the payment matches — not as a claim about what the market charges.

## The arithmetic, checked

- S$105,000 × 2.28% × 7 years = **S$16,758.00** of interest
- Total repayable **S$121,758.00** over 84 months = **S$1,449.50** a month
- The approval states **S$1,450.00** — S$0.50 a month, **S$42.00** over the life

Fortress shows the bank's figure and names the difference. Which month absorbs it is in
the hire-purchase agreement, which has not been supplied.

## What is still missing — one field

**The first payment date is on nothing you have sent, and it is not guessed.**

So `carLoan()` now separates two different jobs:

- **priced** — lender, rate, basis, tenor. Enough to say what the loan *costs*. The
  instalment, the total interest and the true rate are computed from it and are on screen
  now.
- **known** — priced *and* dated. Only this switches on the month-by-month schedule, the
  year-by-year interest and principal, the balance outstanding today, and the equity
  against whatever you think the car is worth. All of those need a calendar to sit on.

Send that one date (or the hire-purchase agreement) and the rest of the card appears.

## A design decision I reversed while building this

My first version let the document win over anything you type into the form. I changed it:
**what you enter wins, and the difference is always shown.** A bank can re-quote, and it
is your file — but an entry form that silently ignores you is worse than no form. Any
difference between your entry and the approval is now reported in both figures.

The test suite caught this: with the document always winning, two code paths had become
unreachable.
